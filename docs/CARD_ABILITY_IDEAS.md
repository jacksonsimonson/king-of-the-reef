# Twenty creature-led ability candidates

Proposal only: no gameplay, creature assignments or balance values change. This replaces the previous eight-idea sketch. All twenty finalists now affect battle only: no rewards, fishing, route previews or post-match recovery. Reviewed the 167-entry catalog, authored regional roles, existing abilities and the relative balancing framework in CREATURE_BALANCE.md. Named hosts exist in the roster; thematic associations are creative interpretations, not zoological claims or approved assignments.

## From forty candidates to twenty

The initial pool contains the twenty finalists and twenty cuts below. First remove rare-condition dependencies, redundant existing mechanics and action/score exploits. Then tighten rules, add ordinary counters and check system coverage. The revised finalists cover movement, defense, delayed growth, attack control, scoring, information, hand/reserve management, adaptation, temporary obstacles, allied maintenance, threat zones and edge sequencing. Only two primarily move creatures. Five former run-level ideas are replaced below; the original forty-concept screening remains recorded as history. These are design-screened proposals, not simulation-tested winners.

Abilities must replace part of a creature's existing power budget, not decorate its already balanced edges for free. Keep basic guides without abilities available. Later regions introduce complexity rather than universal numerical upgrades. Numbers below are provisional experiments; use relative comparisons, not fixed point costs.

## Movement

### 1. Slipstream — Flying Fish, Open Ocean
After its placement edges, optionally step one orthogonal space into an empty safe tile, including a reef. No new edges or entry-terrain triggers; normal final whirlpool processing follows. Choose scoring access, escape or staying put; blocked destinations deny movement. Fits the existing surface-vault role. Compare with Drift Shell/Bounce and reduce existing movement or Double coverage before combining. Last-play reef access is the main risk.

### 2. Towline — Remora, Shoreline
After edges, optionally pull one ally exactly two tiles away into the empty tile between it and Remora. Choose one cardinal direction; Anchor, kelp and unsafe destinations block it. No retriggers. Deliver a holder or rearrange a formation; an occupied gap stops the plan. Fits its attachment-arranger role. Compare with its existing right Hook: adaptable direction, but only one friendly target. Remove redundant reach if necessary.

## Defense and preservation

### 3. Inflate — Pufferfish, Shoreline
Block the first otherwise-successful enemy edge movement against this creature, then spend its visible inflation token for the tide. Friendly movement does not consume it; predation, terrain and self-movement bypass it. A probe followed by a second ordinary push defeats it. Compare with one-use Anchor that permits allied delivery. Reconsider its existing upper/lower Shields so the token does not close every approach. Failed attacks do not spend the token.

### 4. Borrowed Armor — Decorator Crab, Shoreline
Before placement edges, choose one empty side and add a Shield for this tide. No overwriting attacks; no empty side means no bonus. Cover the dangerous approach but leave another opening; the Shield can obstruct friendly delivery. Fits its decorated, guarded-claw identity. Compare with Coral Mail's all-blank-side protection; test replacing the existing fixed right Shield with this choice rather than simply adding armor.

### 5. Spreading Arms — Sea Star, Shoreline
At the start of your next turn after playing it, if it is still on the board, optionally add one Standard edge to a blank side for the rest of the tide. Once only; adding the edge does not activate it or retrigger placement. The edge provides normal directional defense and can be disabled by Shock. Choose a future protected approach, but accept that it may block friendly delivery too. Fits the tube-foot arranger as delayed growth rather than post-match healing. Compare with Spear Shell: one delayed side instead of immediately filling all blanks. Removing it before maturation prevents growth; final-play deployment gives no bonus. Keep its current useful setup edges.

## Attack decisions

### 6. Focused Strike — Mantis Shrimp, Shoreline
Before edges, designate one Double edge to ignore edge defenses for this placement. Bulwark, Anchor, terrain and occupied destinations still stop it normally. No Double edge means no grant. Open one defended lane, rather than penetrate in every direction. Fits its concentrated punch. Compare with Piercing, which affects more attacks and defenses. Retain limited direction coverage and do not add free wide-area or lethal coverage.

### 7. Pack Hunt — Orca, Open Ocean
Its Bite ignores edge defenses when the enemy target is beside another allied creature, excluding the acting Orca. Bulwark and normal Bite destination requirements remain. No additional attack. Set up a flanker; opponents can move that ally or block the Bite exit. Any ordinary ally qualifies, not only Orcas. Compare with conditional Piercing and trim its existing pursuit/setup package if kills become too reliable. Friendly targets never receive this bonus.

### 8. Gentle Giant — Whale Shark, Open Ocean
Before edges, optionally skip one offensive edge for this placement. Keep its defensive marker. This skips the entire selected Wave, not individual friendly targets. Decide whether the sweep is worth scattering allies, or hold safely instead. Fits its sheltered filter-sweeper role. Removing compulsory friendly fire is real power even without adding attacks. On its current one-attack card, the decision is sweep or hold.

## Scoring

### 9. Shoal — Clownfish, Shoreline
Assign to Clownfish, removing its left Standard edge to retain an upward guide and pay for scoring. Keep Minnow, Anchovy, Sardine and Goby unchanged. Score two on a reef while beside at least one ally, otherwise one. No stacking with Sovereign or multiple neighbors. Spend another creature supporting the holder or use it elsewhere; displacing either member breaks the bonus. Compare with unconditional Sovereign. Preserve weak defenses and indirect access, and keep the other basic guides simple.

### 10. Reef Network — Staghorn Coral, advanced Shoreline
While off a reef and beside a reef occupied by an ally, contribute one point. On a reef, use ordinary reef scoring instead, never an additional Network point. Maximum one off-reef Network point per owner across copies; a Sovereign-bearing host cannot supply it. Taking the occupied reef or moving/removing the coral breaks the connection. This replaces the earlier two-held-reefs bonus, which usually increased an already-winning margin. Fits a branching colony supporting a reef holder. Compare with Shoal: the support can be placed normally, so remove its left Spines and keep exposed approaches. Test against direct reef deployment and scoring combinations before enabling.

## Information, draws and hand disruption

### 11. Sonar — Bottlenose Dolphin, advanced Shoreline
On play, reveal the next two opposing reserve cards in order to both players. Keep their identities revealed if drawn; shuffling can change order but does not erase knowledge. With one reserve reveal one; with none, reveal one hidden hand card if possible. Plan for incoming threats without selecting or removing them. Fits a cooperative scout. Compare with Revelation: more forward information, less immediate certainty. Keep normal edges useful when everything is known.

### 12. Relay — Humpback Whale, Open Ocean
After placement, inspect your next two reserve cards, choose the replacement draw, and leave the other on top. With one card draw normally; with none do nothing extra. No added hand size or plays. The opponent sees a choice occurred, not hidden identities. Fits a coordinating song. Compare with Rally/Dredger Net; unconditional selection should cost some of its extensive sweep coverage. Its board role must still work on the last play.

### 13. False Lure — Anglerfish, Open Ocean
After placement, reveal one chosen hidden opposing hand card. Optionally exchange it with the opponent's next reserve card, putting the revealed card at the bottom. The incoming card stays hidden. Without a reserve, reveal only. Delay a known threat at the risk of supplying a better one. No skipped enemy play. Compare with Revelation plus hostile Dredger Net; keep tight edge coverage. Initially cap forced exchanges at one per owner per tide across copies.

## Adaptation and board objects

### 14. Mimicry — Pelagic Octopus, Open Ocean
Before placement edges, optionally copy one adjacent creature's edge effect into one of your blank sides. Choose the destination direction; insert the new edge into clockwise resolution order, retaining it for the tide. No ability copying or overwrite. First prototype allows only Standard, Hook or Shield, excluding lethal and wide-area chains. Borrow a useful tool while accepting its friendly-fire consequences. No neighbor means no copy, so retain an independent base role. Reduce the current diver's edges to leave at least two blank sides. Compare with a selective one-edge charm.

### 15. True North — Compass Jelly, Bermuda
Before placement, choose any of four orientations for its edges. Rotate directions while preserving stored resolution order; do not rotate ownership, terrain or ability graphics. Aim its effects, but also rotate defensive openings and later movement origins. Fits its turning-compass role. Compare with Nautilus Dial; four orientations are significantly stronger than one clockwise step. Simplify the existing Swap/Shock/Swap chain before granting this flexibility.

### 16. Afterimage — Phantom Shark, Bermuda
When an enemy edge removes it, leave a one-use decoy on its vacated tile if that tile is empty and safe. The decoy scores nothing, has no edges/abilities and blocks direct placement. Incoming movement destroys it and continues normally. Otherwise it expires after the removing side's next placement resolves, or at tide end. No token on occupied tiles, rocks or trenches, or after non-enemy removal. Leave a temporary obstruction without retaining reef points; ordinary movement clears it. Fits a fading phantom. High implementation cost: separate token rendering, saves and expiry. Keep normal edges useful when a late death produces no benefit.

## Support, zones, hand choices and sequencing

These replace the former Voyage traits. They work within Quick Match and Voyage combat under the same rules. All changes expire at tide reset; no school mutations, rewards or recovery carry into later encounters.

### 17. Grooming — Cleaner Wrasse, advanced Shoreline
After its edges, choose one orthogonally adjacent ally. Clear its Shock if present and optionally rotate its edges one quarter-turn clockwise or counterclockwise. No edge activation or other status removal; it stays on the same tile. Aim an ally's defenses or open a side for friendly delivery even when nobody is shocked. Fits a lateral support fish working among larger neighbors. Compare with Nautilus Dial applied to a board ally plus narrow cleansing. Must retain normal setup edges when alone. The chosen orientation also exposes another approach; it is not blanket protection.

### 18. Snare Line — Rust Lobster, Bermuda
After its edges, mark one adjacent empty safe tile. Until the start of your next turn or tide end, the first enemy placed directly on that tile skips its first offensive edge; other edges and full-card abilities resolve normally. The marker is consumed by that placement even if the creature has no offensive edges. Movement through the tile is unaffected. No scoring, damage or displacement from the marker itself. One active marker per owner, replacing an older one. A visible warning creates a short-lived threat zone; rivals can use another tile, expend a harmless placement or lead with an expendable edge. Fits the current rusted claw-trap role. Compare with a restricted, avoidable Ambush rather than a second rock. Declare skips from stored edge order before self-movement and record them in the preview. Ordinary legal placement is never denied.

### 19. Muster — Seahorse, advanced Shoreline
After placement and its normal replacement draw, optionally exchange one other unplayed hand card with the top reserve card. Send the selected card to the bottom; the incoming card stays hidden from the opponent. With no reserve or no other unplayed card, no exchange occurs. No extra draw, play or recycling of played cards. Choose to keep a known card or risk a new one; the newly filled placement slot cannot be exchanged by this effect. A nurturing school-organizer is a creative extension of its vertical tether role. Compare with Dredger Net: no selection among three, but an innate effect. Unlike Relay, this repairs an existing hand slot rather than choosing the automatic replacement. Preserve its ordinary board value near reserve exhaustion.

### 20. Battle Plan — Map Serpent, Bermuda
Before placement, choose to resolve its offensive edges in their existing order or the exact reverse. Directions and defensive sides do not rotate. All edges still attempt normally; no repetitions, arbitrary reorder or extra plays. Plan whether the Swap, Hook or pursuit happens first, knowing that self-movement changes the origin of later edges. Fits the charted-route arranger. Compare with True North: sequencing instead of orientation. Its existing Swap/Hook/Follow chain is a high-risk host and likely needs one edge removed. Preview the complete result; keep normal terrain-before-edges and ability timing unchanged. Stress the combination with granted Wake and orientation charms before assignment.

## Twenty cuts from the forty-candidate pool

| Cut/deferred concept | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Backwash: retreat while dragging a neighbor | Third movement variant; compound failure cases overlap Bounce/Swap. |
| Breakwater: protect adjacent allies from the boundary | Too narrow on sparse, large boards. |
| Territorial: Anchor when isolated on a reef | Conditional duplicate, with oppressive last-play potential. |
| Broadside: chosen axis attacks twice | Edge-order, charm and lethal chains are too complex for this expansion. |
| Crown: always score two | Already Sovereign. |
| Hatchling Swarm: create scoring copies | Undermines placement budgets and adds token ownership complexity. |
| Royal Territory: create an extra reef | Changes the three-objective scoring model too broadly. |
| Electric Battery: bonus after Shock | Status dependency; preserve the existing full-Shock Eel gimmick. |
| Detox: remove Shock only | Too often empty; Grooming adds useful orientation control even without Shock. |
| Thermal Feeder: vent-dependent benefit | Repeats the explicitly rejected rare-terrain dependency. |
| Current Rider: channel-only strength | Depends on sparse random specials. |
| Burrow: deploy on rocks | Niche exception rather than dependable identity. |
| Blood Frenzy: extra play after killing | Breaks 4/5/6 placement budgets and amplifies initiative. |
| Time Theft: skip the enemy turn | Denies interaction, especially at the end. |
| Revenge Blast: kill adjacent creatures on death | Punishes ordinary counterplay and creates cascades. |
| Charm Echo: duplicate/refund charms | Risks loops and distorts the three-slot economy. |
| Pearl Bank: passive shells from carrying a clam | Rewards unused backpack copies; all income effects are now outside the battle-only scope. |
| Master Angler: automatic catch | Removes fishing rather than supports it. |

## Recommended prototypes and guardrails

Prototype Inflate, Gentle Giant, Sonar, Shoal and Grooming first: defense, collateral control, information, scoring and allied maintenance. Next consider Towline, Relay and Muster. Leave Afterimage, Mimicry, Snare Line and sequencing/orientation choices until preview and snapshot handling is ready. This is advice, not approved implementation scope.

Each host needs a fresh role review and an explicit keep/remove decision on current edges. Compare its old version, a same-role peer and an ordinary common alternative. Test sparse openings, allied delivery, defended enemy holders, final plays in both seat orders, all three board sizes and both tides. Stress Reef Beacon plus scoring, Anchor plus Inflate, and charms plus copying/orientation. Evaluate all benefits inside the battle; no permanent school changes or outside rewards are in scope.

Specify ordering against terrain, edges, Wake, whirlpools and replacement draws before implementation. Movement does not retrigger abilities; Shock disables edges, not innate abilities; tide resets clear temporary tokens and modifications. No extra placements, infinite loops or scoring decoys. Optional choices need previews, cancel/skip behavior, AI parity and stable checkpoints. Delayed growth, snare expiry, optional draws and ordering choices require explicit turn-boundary, exhausted-side, duplicate-copy and reload tests. The original eight-idea sketch is superseded: two movement candidates remain, Formation becomes Shoal, Relay is retained, and the other concepts are cut or replaced.

## Battle-only refinement

Regrowth, Cleaning Station, Wreck Salvage, Patient Guide and Chartmaker are removed from the shortlist because their benefits occurred outside combat. Their replacements are Spreading Arms, Grooming, Snare Line, Muster and Battle Plan. The count remains twenty. No new abilities are implemented.
